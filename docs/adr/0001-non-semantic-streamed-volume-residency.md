# Keep streamed Voxel Volume residency separate from scene meaning

The shaped outcome in [#116](https://github.com/PetrSeifert/voxel-nexus/issues/116) keeps volume membership fixed and treats residency as availability of materialized and derived data, without changing logical contents or publishing scene revisions. A streamed publication supplies immutable volume metadata and CPU content sources whose identities and parameters are bound to the scene identity. Sources are deterministic within a process run and total over declared bounds. Compact versioned edits preserve current values and the older values needed by live views; views retain reconstruction information rather than pinning complete voxel payloads.

Keeping every CPU payload would preserve reads but fail CPU residency evidence. Instead, non-resident reads rebuild transparently through a shared bounded cache, and non-resident edits evaluate individual coordinates without changing the residency selection. Shared copies are keyed by scene identity, volume identity, and Voxel Volume Content Version, so unrelated scene edits do not invalidate them. One independently ordered Voxel Residency Selection drives the frontend and both Render Paths. Newest-only preparation, atomic installation, coverage-aware camera acceptance, and residency-aware handoff preserve coherent frames while memory is reclaimed after readers finish.

Streamed scenes use sparse storage. Their compute Render Path uses the explicitly selected brickmap representation; pairing a streamed scene with dense compute must produce a typed rejection. Raster participates fully in switching, eviction, reload, and revision correctness. This outcome does not prove residency independence across Storage Tiers or compute representations. Existing dense/sparse canonical comparisons remain regression evidence. Detailed streaming architecture, hysteresis, durable persistence, networking, and gameplay are deferred. Exact byte caps require the profiling follow-up recorded in #116 before milestone specification.
